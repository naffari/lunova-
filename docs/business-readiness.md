## 3. The three things that could actually ruin you

Not "risks." These are the ones with a path to losing the truck or the house.

### 🔴 1. Financed truck on a personal auto policy

**This is your most dangerous exposure — worse than the missing liability
insurance.** Personal auto policies exclude business use. Haul a paying
customer's junk, wreck on the way to the transfer station, and:

- the claim is **denied**,
- the truck is gone,
- **you still owe the lender every remaining payment.**

That is not a probability, it is a mechanism. Commercial auto in Missouri for a
single truck runs roughly **$150–250/month [VERIFY]**. Get it before the first
paid job. Your lienholder almost certainly requires comprehensive and collision
anyway — check your loan terms.

### 🔴 2. No liability insurance, doing damage-prone work

You are hauling other people's property and running machinery near their
property. A dropped couch through drywall, a mower throwing a rock through a
window, a load shifting off an open trailer on the highway. With no entity and
no GL, that claim lands on **you personally**.

General liability for landscaping + junk removal with no employees runs roughly
**$40–70/month [VERIFY]**. Online carriers issue a certificate **same day**.

### 🔴 3. The background-check claim is live and false

You answered "informal only." The site currently tells homeowners:

> *"Every crew member is checked before their first job. Nobody unvetted enters
> your home."*

That is a claim a customer relies on when deciding to let a stranger into their
house, and it is not currently true. Two acceptable fixes:

- **Run real checks on both of you.** ~$20–60 each, results in 1–3 days, and it
  becomes a genuine selling point you can name. Recommended.
- **Set the flag false** in `src/app/constants/credentials.ts` until you do.

There is no third option. This is more urgent than the insurance flag, because
insurance is being correctly *withheld* while this is being actively *asserted*.

## 4. Launch sequence — legally taking jobs in 5 business days

Everything here is doable online or by phone. Costs are real, not padded.

### Day 1 — entity and money (about 2 hours, ~$50)

1. **Form the LLC.** Missouri Secretary of State, Articles of Organization,
   filed online. Roughly **$50** and usually approved immediately.
   Single-member — your girlfriend is unpaid and not an owner, so this stays
   simple. **[VERIFY current fee]**
2. **Get the EIN.** IRS website, free, issued instantly. You need it for the
   bank account.
3. **Open a business checking account.** Bring the LLC filing and EIN. Free
   options at most banks and credit unions.
4. **Point Square/Stripe at the business account**, not your personal one. This
   matters: commingling funds is the single most common way an LLC shield gets
   pierced, and it undoes the entire reason you formed one.

### Day 2 — insurance (about 2 hours, ~$400–800 up front)

5. **General liability.** Get quotes from online carriers (Next, Thimble,
   Hiscox, Simply Business) *and* one local independent broker. Tell them
   **exactly** what you do: lawn care, junk hauling, and that you intend to add
   pressure washing and house cleaning later. A policy written for "landscaping"
   that gets used for hauling can deny the claim. Certificate usually issues
   same day.
6. **Commercial auto.** Same call. Mention the truck is financed and the trailer
   is used for business. Get the trailer listed on the policy.
7. **Ask these five questions explicitly:**
   - Is hauling and disposal of customer property covered?
   - Is damage to a customer's property in my care covered, or excluded?
     (*care-custody-control* — commonly excluded, and it is every job you do)
   - Are my tools covered if stolen off the open trailer? (*inland marine*)
   - Is my girlfriend covered while helping on a job, unpaid and not an owner?
   - What do I need to add before I start pressure washing?
8. **Bind GL + commercial auto.** Then flip `CREDENTIALS.insured = true` in
   `src/app/constants/credentials.ts` — one line, and the trust badges, proof
   strip and FAQ answer come back across all 32 pages at once.

### Day 2 — the two 5-minute fixes

9. **SPF record.** Your domain has DMARC but no SPF, so booking confirmations
   sent through Resend are landing in spam **right now**. Add a TXT record on
   the root domain:
   ```
   v=spf1 include:_spf.resend.com ~all
   ```
   Then verify the sending domain in Resend for DKIM.
10. **Background-check claim** — order the checks, or flip the flag.

### Day 3 — pricing you can actually stand behind

11. **Call the transfer station.** You cannot quote junk work without this. Ask:
    - Per-ton rate, and the minimum charge
    - Hours, and whether they take walk-ins or need an account
    - What they refuse (matches the `cannotTake` list in `servicePolicy.ts` —
      one bad item can get a whole load turned away)
    - Whether there is a separate rate for mattresses, tires, appliances,
      electronics — these usually carry surcharges and are exactly what people
      call a junk hauler about
12. **Define what "a truckload" means for your rig.** Your pickup plus an open
    utility trailer is *not* the same volume as the dump trucks your competitors
    photograph. Measure the cubic yards and price the tiers against that, or you
    will underquote full loads all summer.
13. **Set a lot-size price rule for mowing.** Under ¼ acre = $45. Above that,
    quote by size until the mower is upgraded.
14. **Reprice the landscaping cleanup floor** above $150, by scope and haul
    volume.

### Day 3 — narrow the website

15. The site advertises **eight lines**. You can deliver two. Every one you
    cannot service is a promise that breaks the day someone books it — and a
    referral lost, not just a job. Cut or gate: power washing, window cleaning,
    auto detailing, bin cleaning, commercial cleaning. Cleaning can stay if you
    are ready to take it.

    `SERVICES` in `src/app/constants/services.ts` is a single array and routes
    derive from it. This is hours, not days.

### Day 4–5 — kit and go

16. Junk kit: ratchet straps, hand truck/dolly, tarps, gloves, PPE, a broom.
    **~$250.**
17. Buy the mower if cash allows (Part 6).
18. Paste `PLACE_ID` and `REVIEW_LINK` into
    `src/app/constants/business.ts`. Your GBP is already verified — this is two
    one-line changes that switch on the map link and the one-tap review link.
19. **Start taking jobs.**

### Sales tax — the question you must not guess at

Missouri and Kansas treat services differently, and **Kansas taxes a wider range
of services than Missouri.** Your site markets six Kansas cities (Overland Park,
Olathe, Shawnee, Lenexa, Leawood, Prairie Village, Kansas City KS). Getting this
wrong means back taxes plus penalties on every job ever invoiced, surfacing two
years later.

Take this list to the CPA verbatim:

- Lawn/landscaping maintenance — taxable in MO? in KS?
- Junk hauling and disposal — each state?
- Residential cleaning — each state?
- Pressure washing of real property — each state? *(for when you add it)*
- Do I need a sales tax permit in both states, and at what nexus threshold?
- Which local rate applies — my base, or the job site?

If any line is taxable, the site prices need a "+ tax where applicable"
treatment and the booking confirmation needs the same.

## 7. After launch — the compounding loop

Once jobs are coming in, three habits matter more than anything else on this
page. All three are free and all three are already wired into the site.

1. **Photos on every job.** `assets-src/work/` is empty, so four declared
   galleries render nothing on every service page. Same angle before and after.
   Drop them in `assets-src/work/<service>/`, run `pnpm images`, and they
   publish. For landscaping and junk removal, before/after is the *entire* sales
   pitch.
2. **Review request within 24 hours.** Text every customer using
   `GOOGLE_BUSINESS.REVIEW_URL`. `api/review-request.ts` already exists and is
   waiting on the link. Your GBP is verified, so reviews start counting
   immediately — and review velocity is a large share of local ranking weight.
   You have zero reviews. The first ten change everything.
3. **Friday money close, 30 minutes.** Log every job, categorize spend,
   reconcile the bank, set aside ~25–30% for tax. Self-employment tax (15.3%)
   plus income tax will be your largest single bill, and quarterly estimates are
   due whether you set the money aside or not.

Then, in rough order: local citations with identical NAP (Bing Places, Apple
Business Connect, Yelp, Angi, Thumbtack, Nextdoor, BBB, KC chamber) — your
backlink audit found **3 links from 1 domain, and that domain is Russian
redirect spam**, so authority is effectively zero. Then Facebook and Instagram,
then add them to `SAME_AS` in `business.ts`.

Track $/labor-hour by line for 60 days. Real numbers will not match the
estimates in Part 2, and the gap is the most valuable thing you learn this year.
